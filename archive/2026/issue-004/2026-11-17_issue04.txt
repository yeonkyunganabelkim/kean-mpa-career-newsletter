JAPAN 2027: GLOBAL RESEARCH & FIELD EXPERIENCE
Kean University, Center for Undergraduate Research and Fellowships (CURF)
Dates: May 21–30, 2027 (10 days)
Route: Kobe → Sendai/Tohoku → Tokyo
Focus: Disaster resilience, public policy and community recovery
Deadline: Thursday, December 17, 2026
Who may apply: Graduate students, and undergraduate juniors or seniors with faculty advisor approval. Limited spots available.
Contact: Kean Center for Undergraduate Research and Fellowships (CURF), curf@kean.edu
A ten-day field experience in three cities, with three perspectives on how communities recover: Kobe (rebuilding after disaster: lessons from the 1995 earthquake), Sendai and the Tohoku region (resilience in action: recovery and community strength after the 2011 earthquake and tsunami) and Tokyo (policy, innovation and governance: national strategies, technology and urban resilience). Participants see disaster recovery in action, explore how different sectors work together, build research skills through site visits, interviews and comparative studies, engage with Japanese universities, experts and community organizations, and learn about volunteerism, nonprofits and public-private solutions.
The flyer invites students interested in careers in public administration and public policy; emergency and disaster management; international relations and development; nonprofit management and community leadership; urban planning and sustainability; and social research and public service. Selection is based on academic performance (GPA), career interests, a statement of interest, and research experience or potential.
To apply, scan the QR code on the flyer, or write to CURF at curf@kean.edu with questions.
Why this may fit you: This one is open to you as a graduate student, and its themes read like an MPA syllabus: emergency management, public policy, nonprofits and community recovery, studied on the ground in places that have rebuilt after disaster. If you have been looking for research or international experience to bring to your next job, start here.
VIEW THE FLYER: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/yeonkyunganabelkim/kean-mpa-career-newsletter/main/assets/opportunities/kean_japan_2027_flyer.png

NEXT GENERATION SERVICE CORPS SUMMER POLICY ACADEMY
The Volcker Alliance
Dates: June 14 – July 3, 2027
Where: One week in Washington, DC (June 14–20), then two weeks (June 21 – July 3) at one of three host schools: UC Berkeley, the University of Minnesota or the University of Washington
Cost: Fully funded: travel, lodging and meals are covered, plus a $1,000 stipend
Deadline: 11:59 p.m. Pacific time, December 15, 2026
Who may apply: Current undergraduate juniors who will be seniors in the 2027–28 academic year; U.S. citizens or permanent residents. This is not open to MPA students.
A three-week professional development program that helps undergraduates explore careers in public service: a week of programming in Washington, DC, followed by two weeks of residential study at a host school of public policy. Students from all majors are encouraged to apply.
Know a Kean undergraduate who would be perfect for this? Pass it along. If you teach, mentor or work with undergraduates, or have a younger colleague or family member at Kean who is curious about public service, this is a generous, fully paid way for them to find out what it looks like.
LEARN MORE & APPLY: https://www.volckeralliance.org/initiatives/next-generation-service-corps-summer-policy-academy

FEDERAL OPPORTUNITIES
----------------------------------------------------------------

One of you asked about federal jobs, so here is a small, separate section, starting with one opening that suits those finishing the MPA. Federal applications have their own rules. Read the qualifications and the "How to Apply" section of each announcement closely, and note that federal resumes are usually longer and more detailed than private-sector ones.

MANAGEMENT AND PROGRAM ANALYST (SEASONAL)
National Park Service, Gateway National Recreation Area
Location: Staten Island, NY
Employment type: Full time, temporary/seasonal: not to exceed 1,039 hours; 2 openings; expected start March–July 2027
Grade and pay: GS-9, $34.85 – $45.31 per hour
Deadline: 11:59 p.m. Eastern time, November 23, 2026
Who may apply: Open to the public. U.S. citizenship required. No security clearance required.
Supports the park's administration with management studies of its work processes, program evaluation, policy and regulatory analysis, organizing information and data, and research and analysis that set out alternatives for decision-makers. Occasional travel may be required. Applicants can qualify through experience or through education: a master's or equivalent graduate degree, or two full years of progressively higher-level graduate education leading to one, in a related field (transcripts are required if you qualify through education).
Why this may fit you: If you are finishing an MPA and wondering how graduate education can translate into a federal position, take a close look at this one. The GS-9 announcement allows applicants to qualify through related graduate education, and the work includes program evaluation, policy and administrative analysis, and organizational processes. One important caveat: this is a temporary/seasonal National Park Service appointment rather than a permanent federal position.
Posting: https://www.usajobs.gov/job/892924100
